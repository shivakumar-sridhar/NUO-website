surgical superintelligence

Think about the best surgeon you've ever heard of.

What makes them exceptional isn't all written in a textbook. It's in their hands: the way they move, the pressure they apply, the way they respond to tissue, and the decisions they make in a split second.

That expertise and skill is real.

But today, it lives inside one person. It reaches only the patients that person can operate on. And when they stop operating, much of that expertise disappears with them.

The gap

Today, 5 billion people lack access to safe, affordable surgery.

9 in 10

people in low- and lower-middle-income countries can't get even basic surgical care.

6%

of the world's operations reach these countries, home to over a third of the world's population.

1 in 4

people who do get surgery face financial catastrophe as a result.

And there aren't enough surgeons, with most concentrated in major cities, far from the people who need them most.

Can we bring world-class surgical care to everyone, no matter where they live?

I believe we can.

The thesis

  1. If we can build surgical intelligence, it no longer has to live inside one person.
  2. If it doesn't live inside one person, it can scale.
  3. And if it can scale, world-class surgery can become affordable and accessible to everyone, everywhere.

That is the thesis behind NOU.

How we build it

To build it, we learn from what expert surgery truly involves: not just the steps in a procedure manual, but the movements, decisions, and adaptations that great surgeons develop over thousands of operations.

We build it with surgeons, not around them.

We won't get there all at once.

First

We build intelligence that assists today's surgeons, helping them perform safer, more consistent procedures.

Next

Autonomous systems take on defined parts of an operation, with surgeons in control and supervising.

Eventually

One expert could extend their expertise across multiple autonomous systems, allowing the world's best surgical care to reach far more patients than one surgeon could ever operate on personally.

This isn't abstract to me.

I come from humble beginnings. Even today, people in my own family can't always get the surgical care they need, because of where they live or what they can afford. I've seen what that gap costs a family: the waiting, the worry, the choices no one should have to make.

And my family is not alone. Billions of people live with that gap every day.

I don't believe where you are born should decide whether you get world-class care.

That is why I'm building NOU: to make safe, world-class surgical care accessible to everyone.
